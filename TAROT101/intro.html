INTRO

A Beginner's Guide to Tarot reading
HOW TO READ TAROT

There is often an assumption that Tarot has a definitive meaning, however most of the reading and learning is done through intuition. Each card reveals meaning through it's use of imagery. Most often, it is recommended that beginner's start with the Rider-Waite deck, designed by Pamela Colman Smith, as it is known for its detailed symbolic illustrations. Tarot reading is as easy as familiarizing yourself with the details of each card, which can be broken down into 3 categories: the suit, imagery, and position.

THE SUITS

In Tarot, the cards can be divided into two main groups: The Major Arcana and The Minor Arcana. The Major Arcana encompasses 22 cards that represent archetypal themes, karmic lessons, and major life themes, while the 56 Minor Arcana cards represent everyday experiences. Furthermore, there are four suits in the Minor Arcana known as: Wands, Cups, Swords, and Pentacles.

WANDS

Wands represent fire. Often these cards are associated with determination, strength, ambition, and action, to name a few. It symbolizes masculine energy, brought forth through fire, and often appears when a thought is forming or when you are seeking solution. It is the first stage of development. The negatives could reveal impulsiveness, lack of direction, and egotistical behaviour.

CUPS

Cups represent water, the element of emotion and intuition. This suit centres around relationships, vulnerability, creativity, and inner awareness. It reflects the emotional undercurrent of a situation. Cups often appear when connection, love, or personal reflection is needed. They invite empathy and imagination, but can reveal escapism, illusion, or emotional overwhelm.

SWORDS

Swords represent air and deal with thought, communication, and truth. It highlights logic, conflict, clarity, and difficult decisions. It often surfaces during moments that demand honesty, boundaries, or critical thinking and emphasize the power of communication and perception. When negatively expressed, they reveal anxiety, judgment, and miscommunication.

PENTACLES

Pentacles represent earth and the tangible aspects of life. In terms of work, money, health, and long term stability, this suit focuses on building, investing, and maintaining what is real and practical. Pentacles reflect patience and progress, emphasizing growth over time. In imbalance, they signal material obsession, stagnation, or fear around resources and control.

CARD IMAGERY & SYMBOLISM

The power of Tarot reading lies in understanding the symbolism in the imagery of each card. This can be broken down into steps:


  1. Look for repeating symbols
  2. Look for movement/direction
  3. Note missing symbols
  4. Use your intuition
  5. Read symbols in context


Symbols appear in many different forms. They can be geometric shapes: circles, spirals, triangles, squares, and crosses. But they can also be animals, natural elements, objects and gestures or postures. The meaning of each element is inherently intuitive and metaphorical. A sun can mean joy and clarity, a square is dependable and grounding. When you read the symbolism of a card, you become more confident and nuanced in your reading.

UPRIGHT VS. REVERSED

When pulling Tarot cards, the position of the card actually matters. When a card is upright, it is a straightforward interpretation. Think of it as the default meaning. When a card is reversed, they urge you to think deeper. This doesn’t mean the opposite of the upright meaning, but encourages the reader to consider hidden truths, denials, and challenges they may be facing.
